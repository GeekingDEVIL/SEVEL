## 1. BOXING — "The Sweet Science"

### How Professionals Teach Boxing

**Sources**: [Boxing Canada Coach Manual](https://boxingscience.co.uk/the-boxers-warm-up/), [England Boxing Handbook](https://spartansboxing.com/blog/basic-boxing-training-the-foundation-every-fighter-needs/), [MittMaster Lesson Plans](https://www.mittmaster.com/)

#### Professional Session Structure (60–90 min)
1. **Warm-Up (10–15 min)**: 3 phases — cardio elevation (skipping 3 min), dynamic mobility (arm circles, hip circles, leg swings, torso twists), shadow boxing (footwork first → add jabs → one-two → full combos)
2. **Technical Instruction (15–20 min)**: Coach demonstrates, breaks down mechanics, students drill
3. **Pad/Bag Work (15–20 min)**: Rounds on timer (3 min on, 1 min rest)
4. **Partner Drills / Sparring (10–15 min)**: Controlled, specific scenarios
5. **Conditioning (5–10 min)**: Circuit work
6. **Cool-Down (5–10 min)**: Light jog, static stretching (20–30s holds)

#### Teaching Progression (Simple → Complex)
Professional boxing curricula follow a strict sequence:

**Phase 1 — Foundations (Weeks 1–4)**
- Proper boxing stance (orthodox/southpaw)
- Guard position and hand placement
- Basic footwork: forward, back, lateral movement, pivot
- The jab (lead hand straight punch) — the most important punch
- The cross (rear hand straight punch)
- Basic 1-2 combination

**Phase 2 — Building Blocks (Weeks 5–8)**
- Lead hook (short range)
- Rear uppercut
- Lead uppercut
- Rear hook
- 3–4 punch combinations (1-2-3, 1-2-3-2, 1-6-3-2)
- Slipping (inside/outside)
- Rolling under hooks

**Phase 3 — Defense & Counters (Weeks 9–12)**
- Parrying jabs and crosses
- Catching hooks
- Shoulder roll defense
- Counter-punching (slip and counter, catch and counter)
- Feinting and drawing reactions
- Combination defense drills

**Phase 4 — Ring IQ & Advanced (Weeks 13–16)**
- Cutting off the ring (controlling space)
- Fighting off the back foot
- Punch selection and timing
- Body shots (integration)
- Controlled sparring with specific objectives
- Round management (pacing, energy conservation)

#### Key Coaching Principles
- **One technique at a time**: Master one punch before adding the next
- **Shadow boxing is king**: Every session starts/ends with shadow work
- **Repetition over variety**: 1000 jabs > 10 different combos poorly
- **Defense from day one**: Guard stays up, chin stays down — non-negotiable
- **Fitness is built through boxing**: No separate "cardio day" — the rounds ARE the cardio

#### Solo Training Viability: ★★★★★ (Excellent)
Shadow boxing, heavy bag work, footwork drills, and conditioning can all be done solo. Boxing is the MOST solo-friendly striking art.

## 2. MUAY THAI — "The Art of 8 Limbs"

### How Professionals Teach Muay Thai

**Sources**: [Impact Athletes 12-Week Curriculum](https://www.impact-athletes.com/members/mtk-curriculum/), [Dynamic Striking Progression Guide](https://dynamicstriking.com/blogs/news/muay-thai-progression), [YOKKAO Muay Thai Course](https://training.yokkao.com/), [MittMaster Muay Thai Syllabus](https://www.mittmaster.com/courses/mittmaster-muay-thai-syllabus), [Bangtao Muay Thai](https://bangtaomuaythai.com/beginners-muay-thai-complete-guide-to-techniques-and-training/)

#### Professional Session Structure (90 min, Thailand format)
1. **Warm-Up (15 min)**: Skipping, shadow boxing, light stretching
2. **Technical Instruction (20 min)**: Coach demonstrates technique, students drill on air
3. **Pad Work (20 min)**: 5 rounds of 3 min with trainer holding pads — the core of Thai training
4. **Bag Work (15 min)**: Practice combinations and power shots
5. **Clinch Work (10 min)**: Partner clinching (knees, sweeps, positioning)
6. **Conditioning (5–10 min)**: Core, bodyweight exercises
7. **Cool-Down & Stretching**: Emphasis on hip flexibility for kicks

#### Teaching Progression — 12-Week Rotating Cycle
Unlike boxing, Muay Thai traditionally has NO standardized curriculum (trainer-dependent). However, modern structured approaches use a quarterly rotating cycle:

**Week 1: Stance, Footwork & Timing**
- Muay Thai stance (more square than boxing, to allow kicks/knees)
- Weight distribution (50/50 vs boxing's 60/40)
- Basic movement: advance, retreat, lateral, angle off
- Rhythm and timing drills

**Week 2: Punches**
- Jab, cross, hook, uppercut (same as boxing but with open-hand strikes)
- Long-range straight punches
- Superman punch
- Emphasis: "Punches set up kicks, kicks set up punches"

**Week 3: Elbows**
- Horizontal elbow (slashing)
- Uppercut elbow
- Downward/diagonal elbow
- Spinning back elbow
- Elbow entry timing off clinch breaks

**Week 4: Knees**
- Straight knee (long-range)
- Curved knee (close range)
- Flying knee
- Knee in the clinch
- Skip knee

**Week 5: Kicks**
- Roundhouse kick (the signature Muay Thai weapon) — shin, not foot
- Teep (front push kick)
- Low kick, body kick, head kick
- Switch kick
- Kick mechanics: hip rotation, shin conditioning

**Week 6: Clinch Work**
- Double collar tie (plum position)
- Single collar tie
- Off-balancing (sweeps from clinch)
- Knee strikes in clinch
- Clinch defense and exits

**Week 7: Defensive Techniques**
- Checking kicks (lifting the shin to block)
- Parrying punches
- Catching kicks and sweeping
- Long guard
- Lean-back defense (matador style)

**Week 8: Combinations & Level Changes**
- Mixing punches + kicks + elbows + knees
- High-low combinations
- Body-head combinations
- 3-weapon combos (punch-kick-knee, etc.)

**Week 9: Counters**
- Counter after check
- Counter after catch
- Counter after parry
- Timing-based counters

**Week 10: Feints & Setups**
- Faking kicks to land punches
- Level-change feints
- Rhythm breaking
- Drawing reactions

**Week 11: Distance Management**
- Long range (teep, jab, roundhouse)
- Mid range (hooks, elbows, knees)
- Close range (clinch, short elbows)
- Transitioning between ranges

**Week 12: Ring Craft**
- Using the ropes/cage
- Controlling center
- Pace and round management
- Fight IQ scenarios

#### Key Coaching Principles
- **Perfect execution over speed**: Beginners must kick slowly with perfect form first
- **Pad work is the heart of Muay Thai**: More important than bag work
- **Both sides**: Train orthodox AND southpaw kicks (Thai fighters switch stances)
- **Shin conditioning is gradual**: Not from day one — build up slowly
- **Clinch is 50% of Thai boxing**: Cannot be skipped

#### Solo Training Viability: ★★★★☆ (Very Good)
Shadow boxing (including kicks, knees, elbows), heavy bag work, teep drills, conditioning all work solo. Clinch work requires a partner — can be substituted with clinch-on-bag and solo knee drills.

## 3. BJJ (Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu) — "The Gentle Art"

### How Professionals Teach BJJ

**Sources**: [Gracie Combatives 36 Techniques](https://www.gracieuniversity.com/Pages/Public/Course.aspx?se=293), [Ronin Athletics — The Gracie Approach](https://roninathletics.com/blog/the-gracie-approach-to-teaching-jiu-jitsu-part-2-of-2-the-curriculum-and-a-new-way-of-sparring), [GymDesk BJJ Curriculum](https://gymdesk.com/blog/bjj-lesson-planning-curriculum)

#### Professional Session Structure (60–90 min)
1. **Warm-Up (10–15 min)**: Shrimping, bridging, guard retention drills, solo movement drills
2. **Technique Demonstration (15–20 min)**: Coach shows 2–3 related techniques, explains concepts
3. **Partner Drilling (15–20 min)**: Students practice shown techniques with progressive resistance
4. **Positional Sparring (10 min)**: Start from specific position, specific objectives
5. **Live Rolling (15–20 min)**: Free sparring from knees or standing
6. **Cool-Down & Questions**: Stretching, Q&A

#### The Gracie Combatives Model (Gold Standard for Beginners)

The Gracie system is the most scientifically structured BJJ curriculum ever created. It teaches **36 essential techniques** divided into **23 one-hour lessons**. The philosophy: studies of real fights show these 36 techniques are used more often and with greater success than all other techniques combined.

**The 23 Lessons / 36 Techniques:**

1. Trap and Roll Escape (Mount) + Leg Hook Takedown
2. Americana Armlock (Mount) + Clinch (Aggressive Opponent)
3. Positional Control (Mount) + Body Fold Takedown
4. Take the Back + RNC (Mount) + Clinch (Conservative Opponent)
5. Punch Block Series 1–4 (Guard) + Guillotine Choke (Standing)
6. Straight Armlock (Mount) + Guillotine Defense
7. Triangle Choke (Guard) + Haymaker Punch Defense
8. Elevator Sweep (Guard) + Rear Takedown
9. Elbow Escape (Mount) + Pull Guard
10. Positional Control (Side Mount) + Double Leg Takedown
11. Headlock Counters (Mount) + Standing Headlock Defense
12. Headlock Escape 1 (Side Mount) + Standing Armlock
13. Straight Armlock (Guard) + Guillotine Choke (Guard Pull)
14. Double Ankle Sweep (Guard) + Headlock Escape 2 (Side Mount)
15. Clinch (Conservative Opponent) + Shrimp Escape (Side Mount)
16. Body Fold Takedown + Kimura Armlock (Guard)
17. Leg Hook Takedown + Punch Block Series (5)
18. Haymaker Punch Defense + Hook Sweep (Guard)
19. Guillotine Defense + Take the Back (Guard)
20. Standing Headlock Defense + Elbow Escape (Side Mount)
21. Pull Guard + Twisting Arm Control (Mount)
22. Rear Takedown + Headlock Escape 1
23. Review & Reflex Development (all 36 techniques)

**Completion requirement**: Each technique must be completed **3 times** to earn the Combatives belt.

#### Positional Hierarchy (Core Teaching Concept)
BJJ teaches positions from most dominant to least:
1. **Rear mount** (back control with hooks) — most dominant
2. **Mount** (sitting on top, chest-to-chest)
3. **Side control** (chest-to-chest from the side)
4. **Knee on belly** (one knee across opponent's abdomen)
5. **Half guard** (top) — partial control
6. **Guard** (bottom — legs wrapped around) — neutral/advantageous bottom position
7. **Turtle** (bottom) — defensive but vulnerable
8. **Bottom side control** — worst position

**Teaching philosophy**: Always teach ESCAPES before ATTACKS from each position. A student who can escape bad positions is safer than one who only knows submissions.

#### White-to-Blue Belt Curriculum (Typical 1–2 years)
- **350+ movements** across all major positions
- **12 essential positions** to master
- Guard: closed guard, open guard, half guard, butterfly guard
- Passes: over-under pass, knee cut, toreando, leg drag
- Submissions: armbar, triangle, kimura, americana, guillotine, RNC, omoplata
- Sweeps: scissor sweep, hip bump, flower sweep, elevator sweep
- Takedowns: double leg, single leg, pull guard, trip from clinch

#### Key Coaching Principles
- **Position before submission**: Establish control, then attack
- **Concepts over memorization**: Understand the "why" — frames, leverage, hip movement
- **Drilling is essential**: 100+ reps of a technique before it becomes instinct
- **Specific sparring before live rolling**: Isolate positions to build confidence
- **Every technique has a counter, every counter has a re-counter**: Chain attacks together

#### Solo Training Viability: ★★☆☆☆ (Limited)
BJJ is the hardest art to train solo. Solo drills exist (shrimping, bridging, guard retention movement, granby rolls, technical standup) but no substitute for a partner. **For the app: Focus on movement drills, positional knowledge (video study), and concept understanding. Pair with solo conditioning specific to BJJ (hip flexibility, grip strength, neck bridges).**

## 4. KARATE — "The Way of the Empty Hand"

### How Professionals Teach Karate

**Sources**: [GymDesk Karate Curriculum Guide](https://gymdesk.com/blog/taekwondo-belt-order), [Global Martial Arts University](https://globalmartialarts.university/2024/04/30/taekwondo-basics-for-beginners/), Shotokan and Kyokushin methodologies

#### Professional Session Structure (60 min)
1. **Rei (Bow) + Mokuso (Meditation)** (2 min): Mental preparation, respect
2. **Warm-Up & Kihon (Basics)** (15 min): Line drills — blocks, punches, kicks in sequence
3. **Kata (Forms)** (15–20 min): Practicing pattern sequences solo
4. **Kumite (Sparring) or Bunkai (Kata Application)** (15–20 min): Partner work
5. **Conditioning** (5 min): Push-ups on knuckles, sit-ups, stretching
6. **Rei (Bow)** (1 min): Close with respect

#### The Three Pillars of Karate
Karate's curriculum is built on three inseparable pillars:

**1. Kihon (Basics) — Foundation of Everything**
Basic techniques practiced in isolation, in lines, moving up and down the dojo:
- **Stances**: Zenkutsu-dachi (front stance), kokutsu-dachi (back stance), kiba-dachi (horse stance), neko-ashi-dachi (cat stance)
- **Blocks**: Age-uke (rising block), soto-uke (outside block), uchi-uke (inside block), gedan-barai (downward block), shuto-uke (knife-hand block)
- **Punches**: Oi-zuki (lunge punch), gyaku-zuki (reverse punch), kizami-zuki (jab)
- **Kicks**: Mae-geri (front kick), yoko-geri (side kick), mawashi-geri (roundhouse), ushiro-geri (back kick)
- **Strikes**: Shuto-uchi (knife hand), uraken (backfist), empi (elbow)

**2. Kata (Forms) — The Encyclopedia of Karate**
Pre-arranged sequences that encode fighting principles. Each kata teaches specific concepts:

| Belt / Kyu | Kata | Key Concept |
|------------|------|-------------|
| White (10th kyu) | Taikyoku Shodan | Basic movement, stance transitions |
| Yellow (9th kyu) | Heian Shodan | Forward/backward movement, basic blocks/strikes |
| Orange (8th kyu) | Heian Nidan | Combinations, kicks integration |
| Green (7th kyu) | Heian Sandan | Speed changes, close-range techniques |
| Blue (6th kyu) | Heian Yondan | Balance, kicks, open-hand techniques |
| Purple (5th kyu) | Heian Godan | Jumping, advanced timing |
| Brown (3rd–1st kyu) | Tekki Shodan, Bassai Dai | Power, hip rotation, advanced stances |
| Black (1st Dan) | Kanku Dai, Jion, Empi | Master-level expression |

**3. Kumite (Sparring) — Live Application**
Progressive sparring approach:
- **Gohon Kumite**: 5-step sparring (attacker does 5 set attacks, defender blocks all + counters on 5th)
- **Sanbon Kumite**: 3-step sparring
- **Ippon Kumite**: 1-step sparring (one attack, one defense + counter)
- **Jiyu Ippon Kumite**: Semi-free sparring (attacker chooses from set, defender reacts)
- **Jiyu Kumite**: Free sparring

#### Belt Progression (Typical Shotokan)
- **White → Yellow (10th → 9th kyu)**: ~3 months. Stances, basic blocks, front punch, front kick, first kata
- **Yellow → Orange (9th → 8th kyu)**: ~3 months. Combinations, stepping, second kata
- **Orange → Green (8th → 7th kyu)**: ~3 months. Kick variety, timing, third kata
- **Green → Blue (7th → 6th kyu)**: ~4 months. Speed, power generation, partner drills
- **Blue → Purple (6th → 5th kyu)**: ~4 months. Advanced kata, controlled sparring introduction
- **Purple → Brown (5th → 3rd kyu)**: ~6 months. Bunkai (kata application), free sparring
- **Brown → Black (3rd → 1st kyu → 1st Dan)**: ~12+ months. All kata proficiency, tournament sparring, philosophy/history

#### Key Coaching Principles
- **Kihon first, always**: 10,000 punches before thinking about sparring
- **Kata is not just choreography**: Every movement has a fighting application (bunkai)
- **Etiquette is non-negotiable**: Bowing, respect, discipline are part of the art
- **One technique, one kill (ikken hissatsu)**: Each strike should have stopping power
- **Both sides**: Practice all techniques on both left and right

#### Solo Training Viability: ★★★★★ (Excellent)
Karate is designed for solo practice. Kihon drills, kata, and basic conditioning are all solo activities. Karate was historically practiced alone. **This is the most solo-friendly discipline alongside boxing.**

## 5. TAEKWONDO — "The Way of the Foot and Fist"

### How Professionals Teach Taekwondo

**Sources**: [GymDesk Taekwondo Belt Order](https://gymdesk.com/blog/taekwondo-belt-order), [Athletic Taekwondo Singapore](https://www.athletictaekwondo.sg/taekwondo-belts), [Taekwondo Training Guide](http://www.taekwondo-training.com/education/taekwondo-belts-and-grading), [Global Martial Arts University](https://globalmartialarts.university/2024/04/30/taekwondo-basics-for-beginners/)

#### Professional Session Structure (60 min)
1. **Opening Ceremony**: Bow, attention stance, recite tenets
2. **Warm-Up (10 min)**: Dynamic stretching (heavy focus on hip/hamstring flexibility for high kicks)
3. **Kicking Drills (20 min)**: The core of TKD training — individual kicks in lines, then on pads
4. **Poomsae (Forms) (10–15 min)**: Pattern practice for current belt level
5. **Sparring Drills / Kyorugi (10–15 min)**: Olympic-style or controlled partner work
6. **Cool-Down & Stretching (5 min)**: Extensive leg/hip flexibility work
7. **Closing Ceremony**: Bow

#### The Geup (Kup) Belt System
Taekwondo uses a 10-level colored belt system counting DOWN to black belt:

| Rank | Belt | Symbolism | Key Requirements |
|------|------|-----------|-----------------|
| 10th Kup | White | Innocence, beginning | Basic stance, attention, bow |
| 9th Kup | White + Yellow stripe | Seed planted | Basic blocks (low, middle, high), front punch |
| 8th Kup | Yellow | Earth/roots forming | Front kick, roundhouse kick, Taegeuk Il Jang |
| 7th Kup | Yellow + Green stripe | Growth beginning | Side kick, back kick basics, Taegeuk Ee Jang |
| 6th Kup | Green | Growth | Turning kick, axe kick, Taegeuk Sam Jang |
| 5th Kup | Green + Blue stripe | Reaching upward | Spinning kicks intro, Taegeuk Sa Jang |
| 4th Kup | Blue | Sky/aspiration | Jump kicks, Taegeuk Oh Jang |
| 3rd Kup | Blue + Red stripe | Approaching mastery | Advanced combinations, Taegeuk Yuk Jang |
| 2nd Kup | Red | Danger/caution | Spinning hook kick, Taegeuk Chil Jang |
| 1st Kup | Red + Black stripe | Pre-mastery | All kicks mastered, Taegeuk Pal Jang |
| 1st Dan | Black | Maturity/mastery | All 8 Taegeuk, sparring, board breaking, theory |

Each rank advancement takes approximately **3 months** of consistent training.

#### The 8 Taegeuk Poomsae (Forms)
Each form builds on the previous, introducing new techniques:

1. **Taegeuk Il Jang** (Heaven): Walking stance, front stance, low block, middle block, high block, middle punch, front kick — 18 movements
2. **Taegeuk Ee Jang** (Lake): High section punch, front kick variations — 18 movements
3. **Taegeuk Sam Jang** (Fire): Knife-hand block, knife-hand strike, back stance — 20 movements
4. **Taegeuk Sa Jang** (Thunder): Side kick, knife-hand guarding block, back fist, palm block — 20 movements
5. **Taegeuk Oh Jang** (Wind): Elbow strikes, hammer fist, jumping techniques — 20 movements
6. **Taegeuk Yuk Jang** (Water): Roundhouse kick to face, spinning techniques — 23 movements
7. **Taegeuk Chil Jang** (Mountain): Palm block, backfist, knee strike, double blocks — 25 movements
8. **Taegeuk Pal Jang** (Earth): Jumping kicks, double kicks, all previous techniques combined — 27 movements

#### Technique Categories
**Kicks (Chagi)** — 70% of TKD training time:
- Ap-chagi (front kick)
- Dollyo-chagi (roundhouse kick)
- Yeop-chagi (side kick)
- Dwi-chagi (back kick)
- Naeryo-chagi (axe kick)
- Biteureo-chagi (crescent kick)
- Twi-yeop-chagi (spinning side kick)
- Dwi-huryeo-chagi (spinning hook kick)
- Twi-dwi-chagi (jumping back kick)
- 540 kick, tornado kick (advanced)

**Hand Techniques (25%):**
- Jireugi (straight punch)
- Deungjumeok (backfist)
- Me-jumeok (hammer fist)
- Palkup (elbow strike)
- Sonnal (knife-hand)

**Blocks (Makki):**
- Arae-makki (low block)
- Momtong-makki (middle block)
- Eolgul-makki (high block)
- Sonnal-makki (knife-hand block)
- Batangson-makki (palm block)

#### Key Coaching Principles
- **Kicks are everything**: TKD's identity is kicking — high, fast, powerful, acrobatic
- **Flexibility is trained daily**: Full splits, high kicks require dedicated stretching
- **Poomsae encodes the curriculum**: Each form is a test of cumulative knowledge
- **Speed over power**: TKD emphasizes snap and speed (especially in Olympic style)
- **Both legs equally**: Switch kicks, stance changes are fundamental
- **Board breaking proves technique**: Not just a gimmick — tests focus, alignment, power

#### Solo Training Viability: ★★★★★ (Excellent)
Poomsae, kicking drills, stretching/flexibility work, and conditioning are all solo activities. TKD is one of the most solo-trainable arts. **The forms (poomsae) progression system maps perfectly to an app-based learning path.**

## 6. MMA (Mixed Martial Arts) — "The Complete Fighter"

### How Professionals Teach MMA

**Sources**: [GymDesk MMA Curriculum Guide](https://gymdesk.com/blog/mma-curriculum), [369 MMA Fit Science-Backed Guide](https://369mmafit.com/en/blog/mma-training-beginners-guide), [Florida MMA Training Structure](https://www.floridagetaways.org/how-is-mma-training-structured-across-different-types/), [District Martial Arts Beginner Guide](https://districtmartialarts.com/the-ultimate-guide-to-mma-for-beginners-everything-you-need-to-know/), [Duane Ludwig Rotating Curriculum](https://dynamicstriking.com/products/how-to-build-a-rotating-curriculum-by-duane-ludwig)

#### Professional Session Structure (90 min)
1. **Warm-Up (10–15 min)**: Dynamic stretching, movement flows, sport-specific drills
2. **Technical Instruction (20–25 min)**: Coach demonstrates technique from one discipline, explains MMA context
3. **Drilling (15–20 min)**: Partner drills with progressive resistance
4. **Positional/Situational Sparring (10–15 min)**: Specific scenarios (back against cage, defending takedown, etc.)
5. **Technical Sparring (10–15 min)**: Light contact, focus on applying learned techniques
6. **Conditioning (5–10 min)**: MMA-specific — ground-up exercises, explosive movements
7. **Cool-Down (5 min)**: Stretching and review

#### The 6 Pillars of MMA Training
MMA isn't one art — it's the integration of multiple. Professional MMA gyms structure around these pillars:

1. **Striking (Stand-up)**: Boxing + kickboxing + Muay Thai
2. **Wrestling (Takedowns & Takedown Defense)**: Freestyle/Greco-Roman + cage wrestling
3. **Grappling (Ground)**: BJJ + wrestling scrambles
4. **Clinch Work**: Dirty boxing, Thai clinch, underhook battles
5. **Transitions**: Striking-to-takedown, ground-to-standing, cage work
6. **Conditioning**: Sport-specific energy system training (5-minute rounds)

#### Structured Beginner Curriculum — Rotating Block Model (12 weeks)

**Block 1: Stand-Up Fundamentals (Weeks 1–3)**
- Week 1: Stance and movement (MMA stance — lower and wider than boxing for takedown defense)
- Week 2: Basic punches (jab, cross, hook) + kick check
- Week 3: Basic kicks (front kick, low kick, head kick) + punch defense

**Block 2: Clinch & Takedowns (Weeks 4–6)**
- Week 4: Clinch control (underhooks, overhooks, pummeling)
- Week 5: Takedowns (double leg, single leg, body lock)
- Week 6: Takedown defense (sprawl, whizzer, cage work)

**Block 3: Ground Fundamentals (Weeks 7–9)**
- Week 7: Positional hierarchy + mount escape + guard basics
- Week 8: Side control escapes + submissions from guard (triangle, armbar)
- Week 9: Back control + rear naked choke + turtle escapes

**Block 4: Integration (Weeks 10–12)**
- Week 10: Striking into clinch transitions
- Week 11: Takedown into ground control transitions
- Week 12: Full MMA scenarios — cage rounds, putting it all together

Each block repeats quarterly with progressively advanced techniques — the **rotating/spiraling model**. Students who join mid-cycle can enter any block (techniques are standalone within each lesson).

#### Progressive Training Timeline (Long-term)
**Phase 1 (Months 1–3)**: General Physical Preparation — build the athletic base
**Phase 2 (Months 4–6)**: Sport-Specific Conditioning — MMA-specific movements and energy systems
**Phase 3 (Months 7–12)**: Integration & Sparring — putting it all together under pressure

#### Typical Weekly Schedule (Beginner)
- Day 1: Striking fundamentals
- Day 2: BJJ / Grappling fundamentals
- Day 3: Wrestling / Takedowns
- Day 4: Conditioning + light sparring
- Day 5: MMA integration (mixing disciplines)
- Days 6–7: Rest / active recovery

#### Key Coaching Principles
- **Don't try to learn everything at once**: Focus on one range/discipline per session
- **Sparring should be TECHNICAL, not fights**: Beginners at 30–50% intensity
- **Build a base art first**: Most coaches recommend starting with boxing or wrestling
- **Transitions are what make MMA unique**: The art is in connecting the ranges
- **Conditioning cannot be separated**: You fight how you train — tired technique = no technique
- **87% retention when curriculum is structured**: Random teaching loses students
- **Cross-training schedule matters**: Striking-Grappling-Wrestling rotation prevents burnout

#### Solo Training Viability: ★★★☆☆ (Moderate)
Striking can be trained solo (shadow boxing, bag work). Grappling and wrestling need partners. **For the app: Focus on the striking pillar, MMA-specific conditioning, movement drills, and conceptual understanding of takedowns/positions. Use visualization and concept study for grappling elements.**

## Implementation Strategy for SEVEL App

### What We Learned From the Research

1. **Every art follows a progressive structure**: Simple → Complex, Defense → Offense, Solo → Partner
2. **Sessions follow a universal template**: Warm-up → Technical instruction → Practice → Application → Cool-down
3. **Solo training is viable for 4 of 6 disciplines**: Boxing, Muay Thai, Karate, TKD are highly solo-friendly
4. **BJJ and MMA have limited solo training** but rich conceptual/knowledge components
5. **Repetition-based mastery is universal**: All arts emphasize drilling a technique hundreds of times
6. **Forms/patterns exist in most arts**: Kata (Karate), Poomsae (TKD), shadow boxing (Boxing/MT), solo drills (BJJ)

### Proposed Curriculum Structure Per Discipline

#### Boxing (16 weeks → 4 Levels)
**Level 1 — Foundations (Weeks 1–4, 12 lessons)**
- Lesson 1: Boxing stance (orthodox + southpaw) + Guard position
- Lesson 2: Basic footwork — forward, backward, lateral
- Lesson 3: The jab — mechanics, power generation from legs
- Lesson 4: The cross — rear hand power, hip rotation
- Lesson 5: 1-2 combination + shadow boxing flow
- Lesson 6: The lead hook — short range, elbow at 90°
- Lesson 7: The rear uppercut — driving up from legs
- Lesson 8: 3-punch combinations (1-2-3, 1-2-6)
- Lesson 9: Basic head movement — slipping inside/outside
- Lesson 10: Basic defense — parrying jabs, catching hooks
- Lesson 11: Putting it together — 3-round shadow boxing session
- Lesson 12: Foundation assessment — all basics in flow

### Mastery System Refinement

Current: Learned (1+) → Practiced (10+) → Mastered (50+, 4+ avg)

**Proposed enhancement:**
- **Unlearned**: Haven't taken the lesson
- **Learned**: Completed the lesson once
- **Drilled**: Practiced 10+ times (deliberate practice logged)
- **Proficient**: 25+ practices, 3.5+ avg self-rating
- **Mastered**: 50+ practices, 4.0+ avg self-rating, completed all related techniques

This 5-tier system gives more granular progress feeling and aligns with how martial arts naturally work — a technique isn't "mastered" until it's second nature.
